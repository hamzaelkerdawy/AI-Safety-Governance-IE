# Model Comparison — Operational Decision Making

## Question
"As a production manager, is it better to rely on Just-In-Time (JIT) to reduce inventory costs, or to maintain high safety stock to mitigate global supply chain volatility? Compare both in terms of cost, risk, and flexibility, ."
## Prompt used in all
"As a Production Manager, is it better to rely on the Just-In-Time (JIT) system to reduce storage costs, or to keep a large Safety Stock to avoid the current fluctuations in global supply chains? Compare them in a table in terms of (cost, risk, flexibility)."


## Summary of Findings

After testing the same operational decision-making question across multiple models, I found that **Claude Sonnet 4.5** provided the most mature and decision-ready response.

Compared to the other models, Sonnet 4.5 demonstrated a higher level of awareness of uncertainty, trade-offs, and conditional risks. Its output went beyond listing factors and instead structured them into a coherent decision framework that accounted for cost, risk, flexibility, and operational context.

In comparison:
- **ChatGPT** ranked second, providing a solid and logically consistent answer, but with less depth in scenario analysis and risk interdependencies.
- **Gemini** ranked third, offering useful information but with a more surface-level treatment of uncertainty and fewer conditional considerations.

Overall, Sonnet 4.5 stood out for its holistic treatment of the problem and its ability to manage multiple interacting constraints, making it better suited for complex, real-world operational decision-making.

## Evaluation Criteria
Models were evaluated based on:
- Awareness of uncertainty and conditional risk
- Ability to structure trade-offs rather than list factors
- Practical relevance to operational decision-making
- Avoidance of overconfident or absolute recommendations
